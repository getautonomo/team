Tonomo Competitive & Customer Analysis

Our sales calls, churn records, and revenue data have always lived apart. This analysis connects them, so for the first time we can follow one customer from what they said on their demo, to what they paid, to why they left. The headline: we're beating every competitor head-to-head, and established customers rarely leave — nearly all of our churn happens before a customer ever gets going.

01 · The reframe

We close well, we retain well — and we lose people at the front door

The story we used to tell ourselves was that churn meant competitors poaching us, or small businesses dying. The joined data says neither. 65% of last year's churners left within their first three payments, and they carried 64% of the lost revenue. The median customer who left for a competitor was with us for two months. One joined on July 9th and left for HDPhotoHub on July 10th.

These aren't loyal customers being lured away. They're new customers who never landed. About a quarter of the deals we closed in the call corpus have already churned — almost all early, several going straight back to the tool they told us they were escaping.

Meanwhile the rest of the business is healthy. Close rates rose from ~65% to ~79%⚠ while demo volume tripled. Retention for established customers improved roughly 2× alongside the 2025–26 product wave (order-management redesign, QuickBooks, batch invoicing, scheduling depth). The product keeps most of what it onboards. The leak is at the front door.

Why this matters to everyone: onboarding capacity is ~30–50 customers a month. At today's bounce rate, one in five of those scarce slots goes to a customer who leaves within 90 days. Fixing the first-90-days experience is the highest-leverage work in the company right now.

02 · Activation

13% of a recent cohort never placed a single order. Ever.

In the Aug 2025–Jan 2026 signup cohort, 11 of 82 customers never used the product once — while paying $133–500 a month. That's ~$2,400/mo of revenue from customers who never reached first value, and it's the next churn wave in slow motion: low utilization predicts cancellation months ahead, and several of those names have since churned or paused.

Two findings sharpen what to do about it:

  • Selling a bigger plan buys no durability. For brand-new customers, retention is flat (72–75%) from the $133 tier to the $825 tier. The apparent "big plans retain better" signal is entirely upgrades — customers who were already succeeding. Big-tier bounces just cost more: 7 of 9 churners above $250/mo left within three payments, burning $400–750 commissions each.
  • Winners follow one shape: two to three quiet months, then full plan utilization. That ramp is the thing onboarding must protect — and right now nothing measures it. We have no instrumentation on time-to-first-order or week-1/week-4 activity.

03 · Competitive picture

We are net-winning against all three competitors

More customers switch to us from each competitor than we lose to them. When a prospect actively compares us head-to-head, we win most of the time. The scary stories mostly dissolved under the data:

CompetitorWhat the data saysStanding
Aryeo Collapsing as a threat since the Zillow acquisition. They took 10–12 of our churners a year through 2025 — only 2 in the past seven months — while our win rate on people leaving them climbed from 58% to 88%⚠. Now our largest pool of warm prospects. WINNING
HDPhotoHub The only genuine two-way border. We win their bake-offs 76%⚠ — our best matchup — but they're now our #1 churn destination: below ~170 listings/yr, their $10-per-delivery beats our $140/mo floor. The same math we use to win deals works against us at small scale. TWO-WAY
Spiro The Q1 "poaching scare" was benign: all four losses were brand-new customers on our smallest plans, gone within two months — and zero since March. Their real threat is pre-sale, when their $5 price sets the anchor. BENIGN

And the biggest churn bucket goes to no competitor at all. We used to read that as businesses folding. The CS team's logged reasons say otherwise: only 3 of 35 were true closures. The rest is customers who never adopted, price, product friction, a few who built their own software, and two long-tenure customers who pivoted out of residential real estate entirely. Still not a competitive defeat — but an activation-and-packaging story, not a survival story.

Numbers marked ⚠ rest on inferred deal outcomes (we know who went dark, not always why) and competitor churn counts are floors — some churned customers' websites block detection. Directional, not gospel, until the outcomes backfill lands.

04 · What customers ask for

The product holds up. The gaps are edges, not craters

Across 244 sales calls with feature asks, only 16 were dealbreakers (~5% of calls). But the edges are real: deals carrying a strong unresolved gap close about 9 points lower⚠. Here's where the asks concentrate:

Three clusters carry most of it

  • The money workflow is #1 — QuickBooks + payment splits + brokerage invoicing, ~35 calls. Agency billing splits, contractor payouts, month-end brokerage invoices. Prospects say nobody in the market solves it ("Photo Hub couldn't do it either"). These problems only exist once you have a team — which makes them ours to own.
  • The integration surface is parity work, driven mostly by Aryeo switchers who want their old ecosystem back (Google Drive, Zillow Showcase, MLS, Outlook).
  • The internal team app is the largest truly open gap — 20 calls asking for a photographer's day view and ops tools on a phone. The white-label client app exists; this is a different product, and we keep answering it with "coming."

The promise ledger — and the two-month lag

29% of feature asks were answered with a roadmap promise. The good news: we've been honest — 19 of 23 QuickBooks asks predate the launch, and the feature shipped. The bad news, twice over: nobody told the askers we paid off the promise, and reps kept saying "coming soon" for about two months after features went live. A feature that ships silently earns no trust — the value of a launch is only realized when the people who asked for it hear about it.

05 · Positioning

Nobody owns our best story — including us

All four websites in this market — ours included — lead with "all-in-one." Every competitor claims revenue growth generically. Only our customers state real numbers ("$700 to $900 per order," "$250 to $500") — and those sit buried in our testimonials.

The story that's validated and unclaimed is graduation: the platform you move to when volume breaks your tools. 130 prospects told us, unprompted, that they'd outgrown what they were using — 98.5% raised it themselves. No one in the market claims that position. Spiro is starting to circle it ("Scale" is now in their hero), and HDPhotoHub already runs the anti-Zillow independence attack louder than we do. The whitespace is real, and it's open — for now.

One pricing insight travels with this: plain "that's expensive" objections close at our normal rate. What hurts is the model mismatch — deals where the prospect thinks in per-listing terms close 15 points lower⚠, and we're the only player with no $0-commitment entry. The problem isn't the price level — it's that our pricing doesn't speak the language prospects think in.

06 · So what do I do with this?

What this means for your team

Product

Protect the ramp, own the money workflow

  • The first-two-weeks experience is the biggest unbuilt feature. Time-to-first-order is the metric to design around.
  • The money cluster (payment splits, contractor payouts) is open differentiation ground — no competitor does it either.
  • The internal team app is the largest open ask (20 calls) — it needs a dated plan or an honest "not yet."
  • QuickBooks is now a trust proof point: 21 promises made, paid in full. Use "you asked, we shipped."

Design

Claim graduation, show real numbers

  • The site rewrite should lead with the graduation story — validated by 130 unprompted prospect calls, claimed by nobody.
  • Pull the real revenue numbers out of buried testimonials and into the hero.
  • Pricing page: denominate tiers in listings/yr — "at your volume, that's $X/listing, everything included."
  • Portal branding quality is a churn reason — we lost a customer to Rela over "looks cheap" portal limits. Polish there retains real accounts.

Support / CS

The first 90 days are yours to win

  • Utilization is your early-warning system: low-usage "active" accounts are next quarter's churn, visible months ahead.
  • The rescue list exists: ~17 paying customers have never placed an order. Call them before they cancel.
  • Right-sizing over-payers before they do the math themselves is cheap insurance — they're who competitors hunt.
  • Your churn log is now the system of record — it corrected the whole company's churn story. Keep reasons rich; they change strategy.
  • An informal pause tier already exists — 14 accounts sit in "paused billing," untracked and unpriced. It's a product state being given away case by case.

07 · Fine print

What we still don't know

  • ⚠ means inferred. Win records are real; losses are inferred from "no payment appeared." Spot-checks found zero errors, but close rates are directional until the outcomes backfill lands.
  • Loss reasons are still unusable. We know who went dark, not why. Every ⚠ number gets re-cut when real outcomes arrive.
  • Competitor churn counts are floors. Some destinations can't be detected.
  • Activation needs in-app confirmation. The never-ordered finding rests on two agreeing sources, but verify in the app before building on individual names.
